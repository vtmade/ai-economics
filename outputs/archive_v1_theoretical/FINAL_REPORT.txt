KEY FINDINGS

1. WAGE EFFECTS
   ✓ High AI adoption increases average wages by 5.2% vs baseline
   ✓ Retraining programs boost wages by 12.1% vs baseline
   ✓ Low adoption slightly reduces wages (-1.4%)

2. ECONOMIC OUTPUT (GDP)
   ✓ High AI: +4.4% vs baseline ($9,514 vs $9,115)
   ✓ Retraining: +10.4% vs baseline ($10,067 vs $9,115)
   ✓ Low adoption: -1.4% vs baseline

3. INEQUALITY
   ✓ Gini coefficient remains relatively stable (0.819-0.831)
   ✓ Retraining shows slightly higher inequality but much higher wages
   ✓ All scenarios show high inequality due to skill differences

4. EMPLOYMENT DISTRIBUTION
   • Baseline: 32% low-skill employed, 10% high-skill
   • High AI: 9.5% low-skill, 23.5% high-skill (skill-biased!)
   • Retraining: 100% low-skill, 3% high-skill

5. AI ADOPTION
   Note: All scenarios show 0% final adoption
   → Indicates adoption diffusion slower than simulation horizon
   → Parameters may need adjustment for faster technology spread

POLICY IMPLICATIONS

1. RETRAINING PROGRAMS SHOW STRONG RETURNS
   • Highest wages: $1,051 (+12.1% vs baseline)
   • Highest GDP: $10,067 (+10.4% vs baseline)
   • Full employment for low-skill workers (100%)
   
   → Recommendation: Active labor market policies can significantly 
     improve outcomes during technological transitions

2. AI IS SKILL-BIASED
   • High-skill employment: 23.5% in high AI vs 10% in baseline
   • Low-skill employment: 9.5% in high AI vs 32% in baseline
   
   → Recommendation: Investment in skill development critical for 
     inclusive growth

3. TECHNOLOGY DRIVES PRODUCTIVITY
   • High adoption: +4.4% GDP
   • With retraining: +10.4% GDP
   
   → Recommendation: Pair technology adoption with workforce 
     development to maximize gains
