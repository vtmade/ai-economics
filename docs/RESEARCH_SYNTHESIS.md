# Research Synthesis: AI Labor Market Impact

## Summary of Major Research Findings (2023-2025)

### 1. McKinsey Global Institute (June 2023)

**"The Economic Potential of Generative AI"**

**Key Findings:**
- **Economic Impact**: $2.6-4.4 trillion annually across 63 use cases
- **Productivity Growth**: 0.5-0.9 percentage points annually in US through 2030
- **Work Time Automatable**: 60-70% of current employee time
- **Expertise Automation Jump**: 34 percentage point increase in technical automation potential
- **Management Automation**: Increased from 16% (2017) to 49% (2023)

**Who's Most Affected:**
- **High-wage knowledge workers** (paradigm shift from previous assumptions)
- **75% of value** in: Customer operations, marketing/sales, software engineering, R&D
- **Top sectors**: Banking, high tech, life sciences

**Job Displacement:**
- Office support: -1.6 million jobs
- Customer service: Significant losses
- Food services: Major displacement
- **Job Gains**: Healthcare, STEM fields

**Workforce Transformation:**
- 40% of companies expect >20% workforce reskilling
- Only 8% expect >20% workforce reduction
- Emphasis on transitions over separations

---

### 2. Anthropic (2024)

**"Economic Policy Responses to AI"**

**Policy Framework - 9 Categories by Urgency:**

**Nearly All Scenarios:**
1. Workforce training grants ($10,000/year per trainee)
2. Tax incentives for human capital vs. capital expenditure
3. Corporate tax reforms closing profit-shifting loopholes
4. Accelerated AI infrastructure permitting

**Moderate Disruption:**
5. Trade Adjustment Assistance-style programs
6. Taxes on computational tokens/AI resources

**Severe Disruption:**
7. Sovereign wealth funds capturing AI returns
8. Value-added tax (VAT) adoption
9. Business wealth taxes

**Three Economic Stages:**
1. **Modest effects** → Workforce development
2. **Measurable wage declines/job losses** → Fiscal support
3. **Dramatic displacement** → Wealth redistribution

**Key Insight:**
"Users are increasingly likely to delegate full tasks to Claude, 'collaborating' less" - suggests faster displacement timeline than expected

**Funding Estimates:**
- Automation Adjustment Assistance: ~$700M annually (initial, scalable)
- Workforce training: $10,000 per trainee annually

---

### 3. RAND Corporation (2024-2025)

**"Macroeconomic Implications of Artificial Intelligence"**

**Key Findings:**
- **By 2035**: +$7,000 real per-capita GDP from moderate AI productivity gains
- **Dual Nature**: Productivity enhancement vs. labor displacement

**Labor Market Evidence (2024-2025):**
- **Correlation**: 0.57 between high AI adoption occupations and unemployment gains
- **Demographics**: 22-25 year-olds most affected
- **Occupations**: Software development, customer service, clerical work

**Uncertainty:**
- "Limited visibility into AI's impacts on labor market"
- Concerns: Job displacement, privacy, cybersecurity, bias, inequality

---

### 4. Rabobank (2024)

**"The Economic Impact of AI: Four Scenarios"**

**Framework:**
Uses Dynamic AD-AS model (GDP growth vs. inflation)

**Four Scenarios:**
1. **Dystopian**: No productivity boom, exacerbated inequality, select companies/workers benefit
2. **[Moderate]**: Uneven adoption, productivity gains in specific sectors
3. **Computer Revolution Parallel**: Major investment, rapid development, boosts demand
4. **[Optimistic]**: Widespread adoption, broad productivity gains

**Key Finding:**
- **Employment Focus**: Quality over quantity
- **Demographic Reality**: Western economies "running out of workers before jobs"
- **Displacement Pattern**: Workers forced into lower productivity/income jobs

---

### 5. Epoch AI (2024-2025)

**"The Economic Consequences of Automating Remote Work"**

**Remote Work Automation:**
- **34% of job tasks** can be performed remotely
- **13-18% of occupations** have all top tasks remotely performable
- Existing estimates: ~50% of economy could be remote

**Timeline Estimates:**
- **Median**: 20 years for full automation (conservative)
- **Aggressive**: 1-10 years (many AI field researchers)
- **2030**: Probably too soon for full automation
- **Drop-in remote worker system**: Plausible in 10-15 years

**Economic Impact:**
- **Conservative scenario**: Economy doubles
- **Optimistic scenario**: Economy grows by >10x
- Timeline: Within 20 years under conservative compute scaling assumptions

---

### 6. Tony Blair Institute (November 2024)

**"The Impact of AI on the Labour Market" (UK Focus)**

**Displacement Projections:**
- **By 2050**: 1-3 million UK jobs displaced
- **Peak Displacement**: 60,000-275,000 jobs per year
- **By 2030**: +180,000 unemployment

**Productivity:**
- **Time Savings**: Up to 23% of private-sector workforce time
- Equivalent to annual output of 6 million workers

**GDP Impact:**
- **By 2035**: +6% GDP growth

**Most Affected Sectors:**
- Routine cognitive tasks: Admin, secretarial, customer service
- Banking and finance (data-rich sectors)
- **Least Affected**: Complex manual labor (construction)

**Job Creation:**
- New roles will emerge
- Displaced workers "pulled back into workforce"
- Net effect: Some offset but not full replacement

## Cross-Study Synthesis

### Timeline Consensus

| Timeframe | Expected Impact |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **2025-2027** | Early adoption, limited displacement |
| **2028-2030** | Significant effects visible, 0.5-0.9pp productivity gains, unemployment +100-200K |
| **2030-2035** | Major transformation, GDP +6%, substantial displacement |
| **2035-2050** | Full effects realized, 1-3M displaced, economy doubled (conservative) |

### Automation Potential Consensus

**60-70% of work time is automatable** (McKinsey)
- But only **13-18% of occupations fully automatable** near-term (Epoch AI)
- Focus on **routine cognitive tasks** across all studies
- **Knowledge workers more affected** than previously predicted

### Who Gets Displaced?

**High Risk:**
- Office support/administrative (1.6M jobs - McKinsey)
- Customer service
- Clerical work
- Banking/finance back-office
- Software development (surprisingly!)
- 22-25 year-old entry-level workers

**Low Risk:**
- Healthcare practitioners
- STEM occupations
- Complex manual labor (construction, trades)
- Jobs requiring physical presence and dexterity

### Economic Impact Range

**Productivity Gains:**
- Conservative: 0.5pp annually (McKinsey)
- Moderate: 0.9pp annually (McKinsey)
- Aggressive: 3.4pp annually (McKinsey)

**GDP Impact by 2035:**
- Moderate: +$7,000 per capita (RAND)
- Optimistic: +6% total GDP (TBI)
- Very optimistic: Economy doubles (Epoch AI)
- Extreme: Economy grows 10x (Epoch AI optimistic)

### Policy Consensus

**All Studies Agree:**
1. **Retraining is critical** - but must be scaled ($10K/worker/year minimum)
2. **Graduated response** - policies must match disruption severity
3. **Early intervention** - waiting until crisis is too late
4. **Fiscal mechanisms** needed - VAT, wealth taxes for severe scenarios
5. **Focus on transitions** - not just preventing displacement

## Implications for Simulation Design

### Scenarios Should Reflect:

1. **Conservative (20-year timeline)**
   - Gradual 2% adoption per year
   - Automation: 15-20% of work tasks
   - Policy keeps pace
   - Based on: Epoch AI conservative

2. **Baseline/McKinsey (2030 effects)**
   - 60-70% work time automatable
   - 0.5-0.9pp productivity growth
   - Moderate displacement (office/customer service)
   - Partial policy response
   - Based on: McKinsey central case

3. **Aggressive (Rapid 2025-2030)**
   - Fast adoption in exposed sectors
   - TBI-style 180K unemployment by 2030
   - Knowledge worker displacement
   - Insufficient policy lag
   - Based on: TBI + RAND evidence

4. **Comprehensive Policy (Anthropic)**
   - $10K/worker training grants
   - Graduated policy response
   - VAT + wealth mechanisms if needed
   - Managed transition
   - Based on: Anthropic policy framework

### Key Parameter Updates:

**Automation Rates:**
- Routine cognitive: 70% automatable (up from 50%)
- Non-routine cognitive: 34% augmentable (McKinsey expertise jump)
- Knowledge workers: Higher exposure than previous models

**Timeline:**
- Significant effects by 2030 (not 2040+)
- Peak displacement 2028-2032
- Full effects by 2035

**Economic Gains:**
- 0.5-0.9pp productivity growth baseline
- GDP +6% achievable by 2035 with good policy
- Time savings: 20-25% of workforce equivalent

**Policy Costs:**
- Training: $10,000/worker/year
- TAA-style programs: $700M annually baseline
- Scale with displacement severity

## Research Quality Assessment

| Source | Methodology | Credibility | Key Limitation |
|--------|------------|-------------|----------------|
| McKinsey | Use case analysis, occupational mapping | High - extensive dataset | Industry-funded, may be optimistic on gains |
| Anthropic | Policy analysis, user behavior tracking | High - direct AI deployment data | Self-interested (AI company) |
| RAND | Economic modeling, early empirical evidence | Very high - academic rigor | Limited data (early stage) |
| Rabobank | Scenario planning, macro modeling | High - banking perspective | Less granular on mechanisms |
| Epoch AI | Compute scaling laws, task analysis | High - technical grounding | Highly uncertain timelines |
| TBI | Occupational exposure analysis | Medium - GPT-4 based estimates | Methodology concerns raised |

**Overall Confidence:**
- **High** on direction of effects (automation of cognitive work)
- **Medium** on magnitude (wide ranges)
- **Low** on precise timing (2-3 year uncertainty)

## Conclusion

The research consensus:
1. **Real and substantial** economic impacts coming
2. **2030 is the critical inflection point**, not 2040+
3. **Knowledge workers surprisingly vulnerable**
4. **60-70% of work time automatable** by 2030
5. **Policy response is critical** - $10K/worker minimum investment
6. **Graduated approach** - policies must scale with severity
7. **Net job creation possible** but quality/wage issues remain

**Our simulation should reflect these research-backed parameters rather than theoretical abstractions.**
